# India: Renewable Intermittency, Distribution Reliability and Low-Income Urban Power Access (Fact Sheet for GridKavach "Problem" / "Why now" slides)

Research date: 2026-10-01. Figures drawn mostly from search-result summaries and secondary news coverage of MNRE/Grid-India/MoP data. Spot-check the primary PIB/MNRE pages before putting any number on a slide. Older data (pre-2023) is marked [OLDER].

## 1. Renewable targets and installed capacity

### Takeaway
India hit 50% non-fossil capacity in mid-2025, five years early. Solar reached about 168 GW by Aug 2026, and FY2026 set a record of about 44.6 GW of new solar. Variable renewables are scaling much faster than the firming capacity behind them.

### Cited Findings
- Target: 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; net zero by 2070 — [Mongabay India, Jul 2025](https://india.mongabay.com/short-article/2025/07/india-achieves-50-non-fossil-power-capacity-five-years-ahead-of-target/); [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2144627&reg=3&lang=2)
- India reached 50% non-fossil share of installed power capacity in 2025, five years ahead of the 2030 NDC target — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2144627&reg=3&lang=2); [Mongabay India](https://india.mongabay.com/short-article/2025/07/india-achieves-50-non-fossil-power-capacity-five-years-ahead-of-target/)
- Total non-fossil capacity was 283.46 GW as of 31 Mar 2026 (reported in search summary; the PIB release is the likely origin) — [PIB, Pralhad Joshi](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2250039&lang=1&reg=3)
- Cumulative solar as of 31 Aug 2026: 168.04 GW. Of this, ground-mounted 123.99 GW, grid-connected rooftop 32.59 GW, hybrid solar component 4.83 GW, off-grid 6.63 GW. Wind: 58.52 GW — [MNRE Physical Achievements](https://mnre.gov.in/en/physical-progress/) (as summarised in search results; verify on page)
- FY2026 (Apr 2025–Mar 2026) additions were about 44.6 GW solar (+87.2% YoY) and 6 GW wind (+45.6% YoY), both records — [JMK Research](https://jmkresearch.com/india-installs-record-44-gw-solar-and-6-gw-wind-capacity-in-fy2026/)
- H1 2026 (Jan–Jun) additions were about 26 GW solar (+43% YoY) and 2.9 GW wind (−16% YoY), a record 29 GW in total. Down To Earth credits a "rooftop solar boom" — [JMK Research](https://jmkresearch.com/india-installs-record-26-gw-solar-and-3-gw-wind-capacity-in-h12026/); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/energy/india-adds-record-29-gw-solar-wind-in-first-half-of-2026)
- As of June 2026, RE installed capacity was about 288 GW. Shares: solar about 56%, wind 20%, large hydro 18%, bio 4%, small hydro 2% — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/energy/india-adds-record-29-gw-solar-wind-in-first-half-of-2026)
- 2047 (Viksit Bharat): EY–CII estimates non-fossil energy supply must grow about 40x to meet two-thirds of demand, with primary energy supply tripling to about 35,000 TWh — [EY–CII, Sep 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_in/newsroom/2025/09/india-needs-40-x-surge-in-non-fossil-energy-to-meet-viksit-bharat-2047-vision-eycii-report). NITI Aayog's power scenarios for Viksit Bharat/Net Zero are in [NITI Aayog Vol. 7, Feb 2026](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-02/Scenarios-Towards-Viksit-Bharat-and-Net-Zero-Sectoral-Insights-Power_0.pdf) (not opened; extract exact GW numbers if needed)

### Inferences
- Solar's share of RE capacity is about 56%. Every new GW pushes supply further toward midday, which deepens the evening ramp problem.

### Gaps
- I did not verify an official government "2047" GW capacity target (for example 1,800 GW). Only the EY–CII and ENCIS projections were found. Do not cite a government 2047 GW number without the NITI PDF.
- The search summaries disagree on the date of the 283.46 GW non-fossil figure (31 Mar 2026) and on the RE total (about 288 GW in Jun 2026, RE only). These may measure different things (non-fossil incl. nuclear vs RE incl. large hydro). Confirm on PIB.

## 2. Evening peak / duck curve and non-solar-hour shortages

### Takeaway
India now meets record daytime peaks with almost no shortage. The deficits show up at night, when solar is gone: 2.5 to 5.4 GW short around 22:30–22:45 on record days in Apr–May 2026.

### Cited Findings
- All-time peak demand was 270.8 GW on 21 May 2026, up 26% in 50 days from 214.9 GW on 1 Apr 2026. RE incl. hydro met 34% of the peak — [Down To Earth Power Demand Tracker](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/power-demand-tracker-india-meets-all-time-high-power-demand-of-2708-gw-renewable-energy-share-34)
- On 21 May 2026 the solar-hours shortage was only 0.18 GW, but the night-time deficit was 2.57 GW (about 10:45 pm). Seven states saw shortages, with Haryana the highest. Coal ramped to 184.9 GW in non-solar hours — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/power-demand-tracker-india-meets-all-time-high-power-demand-of-2708-gw-renewable-energy-share-34)
- On 25 Apr 2026 the peak was 256 GW at 3:38 pm, with solar peaking at 80.8 GW earlier that day. The evening shortfall was 4.2 GW at 10:39 pm (25 Apr) and 5.4 GW on 24 Apr. Hydro's share rose from 4–5% in solar hours to 11–12% after 6 pm. The coal fleet can ramp only about 1–3% — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/decoding-indias-record-256-gw-power-peak-demand-dispatch-and-dilemmas)
- Record peak of 257.37 GW met "without shortfall" (May 2026). The record non-solar-hours peak met was 247.21 GW at 22:29 — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/india-meets-record-peak-power-demand-of-257-dot-37-gw-without-shortfall-enn26051904481)
- Business Standard (Jun 2026) headline: "India's solar boom eases daytime demand, but night shortages persist" — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/solar-power-india-grid-gaps-storage-evening-shortages-126060300625_1.html) (paywalled/403; headline only). The same coverage cites an estimate that India needs over 411.4 GWh of BESS by 2031-32 (CEA National Electricity Plan figure; verify).
- Storage under construction: 28.7 GWh ESS and 78.7 GWh pumped storage (Apr 2026) — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/decoding-indias-record-256-gw-power-peak-demand-dispatch-and-dilemmas)

### Inferences
- Slide line: "India never runs short at 3 pm anymore. It runs short at 10:45 pm." Night deficits of 2.5–5.4 GW are what turn into rotational load shedding at the feeder level. Grid-India reports shortages state-wise, and DISCOMs then shed load feeder-by-feeder.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Grid-India daily PSP reports directly. Shortage numbers come via Down To Earth and ETV Bharat. Energy-not-served (MU) figures for evenings were not obtained.

## 3. Rooftop solar / PM Surya Ghar

### Takeaway
PM Surya Ghar crossed about 50 lakh installations by Aug 2026, halfway to the 1 crore homes target (Mar 2027). This creates a large base of distributed, unmanaged midday generation at the neighbourhood level.

### Cited Findings
- Target: rooftop solar on 1 crore households by March 2027 — [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/infrastructure/pm-surya-ghar-scheme-crosses-50-lakh-installations-as-july-delivers-record-506-lakh-additions); [IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/government-schemes/pm-surya-ghar-yojana)
- Progress was 24 lakh households by Dec 2025 ([All India Radio](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/over-24-lakh-households-installed-rooftop-solar-systems-under-pm-surya-ghar-scheme)). By May 2026 it passed 40 lakh beneficiary households and more than 12 GW ([SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2026/05/30/pm-surya-ghar-scheme-solarises-40-lakh-households-accelerating-indias-rooftop-solar-growth/)). By Aug 2026 it crossed 50.06 lakh installations, with July 2026 a record month at 5.06 lakh ([Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/infrastructure/pm-surya-ghar-scheme-crosses-50-lakh-installations-as-july-delivers-record-506-lakh-additions); [NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/pm-surya-ghar-scheme-crosses-50-lakh-rooftop-146.htm))
- Daily installations rose from 5,038 (Oct 2025) to about 16,328 (Jul 2026). About 19 lakh households now get zero bills, and 12 lakh households earned Rs 421 crore selling surplus power — [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/infrastructure/pm-surya-ghar-scheme-crosses-50-lakh-installations-as-july-delivers-record-506-lakh-additions)
- Total grid-connected rooftop solar was 32.59 GW (31 Aug 2026) — [MNRE](https://mnre.gov.in/en/physical-progress/)

### Inferences
- Rooftop PV exports peak at midday, when local feeders are lightly loaded, and generate nothing at the evening peak. A shared community battery can absorb surplus and discharge after sunset. That is the core "why now".

### Gaps
- Figures are from secondary outlets quoting MNRE. The PIB/MNRE scheme dashboard (pmsuryaghar.gov.in) was not fetched.

## 4. Supply hours, load shedding and low-income urban access

### Takeaway
On average urban India gets about 22 hours/day of supply, but two-fifths of urban households face daily outages. Low-income and slum households cope worst: kerosene backup is still common, and slum studies report near-daily blackouts. Much of this data is older than 2023.

### Cited Findings
- [OLDER, IRES 2020] An average household gets 20.6 h/day of grid supply: urban 22 h, rural 20 h. Delhi, Kerala and Gujarat are above 23 h. Rural households in UP, Jharkhand, Haryana, Assam and Bihar face 6+ hours of daily outages. Two-thirds of rural and two-fifths of urban households face at least one outage a day. Survey of about 15,000 households across 152 districts in 21 states — [CEEW, India Residential Energy Survey](https://www.ceew.in/publications/access-to-electricity-availability-and-electrification-percentage-in-india)
- [OLDER, 2019] In a Prayas survey of 3,000 semi-urban/rural households in UP and Maharashtra, about 34% of households, including many low-income ones, still used kerosene lamps as backup — [Prayas Energy Group](https://energy.prayaspune.org/our-work/article-and-blog/electricity-supply-quality-issues-persist)
- [OLDER] More than 80% of slum dwellers studied face daily blackouts. Barriers include lack of legal title, affordability and housing quality — [Factsreports, "Energy Access in Informal Settlements"](https://journals.openedition.org/factsreports/4173) (as summarised; verify study year/city). See also [SPARC Energy Justice for the Urban Poor](https://sparcindia.org/pdf/studies/EnergyJusticefortheUrbanPoorgraphicalReport_webversion.pdf) and the [Mumbai slum survey (ScienceDirect, 2014)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0973082614000507)
- LocalCircles survey headline: "85% of Indian households surveyed face power outages every day" — [LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/power-outage-survey) (online self-selected sample; year not verified, use cautiously)
- [2022] 41% of Indian respondents used an inverter during outages, and only 2% had no backup at all — [Statista survey, 2022](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1311831/household-india-power-outages-coping-strategy-heatwave-high-temperatures/) (online sample, skews affluent/urban)
- Hours of supply by DISCOM and urban/rural are published on the National Feeder Monitoring System, per search results (link not directly obtained).

### Inferences
- Inverters are the private, per-home answer to load shedding, and low-income households often cannot afford them. That is the equity case for a shared neighbourhood battery that protects critical loads (fans, lights, phone charging, medical devices) for everyone on the feeder.

### Gaps
- No post-2023 nationally representative survey of urban supply hours was found. IRES 2020 is the latest robust one.
- No reliable NFHS/NSS statistic on the share of urban households owning inverters was found. No current price benchmark was found for a home inverter plus battery (commonly Rs 15,000–30,000 for a basic 1 kVA plus 150Ah tubular, but this is unverified and should not be cited without a source).
- No post-2023 quantitative study of outages specifically in Indian slums was found.

## 5. Reliability indices (SAIFI/SAIDI) and regulation

### Takeaway
The Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules 2020 make 24x7 supply the norm and require SERCs to set SAIFI/SAIDI standards with compensation. In practice, half of India's DISCOMs publish no outage data at all.

### Cited Findings
- The ERC Rules 2020 mandate 24x7 supply. SERCs specify SAIFI/SAIDI standards, and DISCOMs are liable to compensate consumers for interruptions beyond specified durations or numbers — [Legal Service India](https://www.legalserviceindia.com/legal/article-21253-electricity-rights-of-consumers-rules-2020-a-critical-analysis-of-consumer-rights-and-distribution-licensee-obligations.html); [IJPIEL](https://ijpiel.com/index.php/2020/10/19/where-the-wires-crossed-an-analysis-of-the-draft-electricity-rights-of-consumers-rules-2020/)
- Example SERC implementation: MPERC order specifying SAIFI/SAIDI (20 Jun 2024) — [MPERC](https://mperc.in/uploads/editor/Other%20Orders%20by%20MPERC/MPERC_ORDER_Specifying_Reliability_Indices_SAIFI_AND_SAIDI_20_06_2024.pdf); APSERC Standards of Performance Regulations 2024 — [APSERC](https://apserc.nic.in/pdf/Regulations/38-APSERC%20Standards%20of%20Performance%20Regulations%202024%20-%20215%20EOG%20215%20-%20Electricity-1.pdf)
- Of 72 DISCOMs, only 36 (50%) publish any outage data online, and only 17 report SAIFI/SAIDI. Just 8 have five years of history, 15 report SAIFI/SAIDI monthly and 2 daily — [The Leap Blog, Sep 2025](https://blog.theleapjournal.org/2025/09/a-review-of-outage-reporting-by-indian.html)

### Inferences
- The reliability data gap is itself a pitch point. Feeder-level metering and outage logging (which GridKavach would need anyway) also produces the SAIFI/SAIDI evidence regulators are asking for.

### Gaps
- No actual SAIDI values for metro DISCOMs (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru) were retrieved.
- The details of the 2024 amendments to the ERC Rules (rooftop solar and EV connection timelines) were not verified in this session.

## 6. Distribution transformer failures and AT&C losses

### Takeaway
About 10% of India's distribution transformers fail every year (about 1.3 million). Failure rates exceed 20% in some northern states, against about 0.5% at private utilities like Tata Power-DDL. AT&C losses have fallen to roughly 15–16% (FY25).

### Cited Findings
- National DT failure rate is about 10%, roughly 1.3 million failures a year. Kerala is at 1.9%, some northern states exceed 20%, and private utilities (Tata Power-DDL, CESC) are at about 0.5%. Moisture ingress is cited as the cause in about 90% of failures. CEA issued standardisation guidance in Nov 2025 — [Mercom India](https://www.mercomindia.com/cea-steps-in-as-distribution-transformer-failures-hit-1-3-million-a-year); [SolarQuarter, Nov 2025](https://solarquarter.com/2025/11/18/cea-pushes-for-standardization-to-reduce-1-3-million-distribution-transformer-failures-nationwide/)
- [OLDER, 2018-19] UP had about 18 lakh DTs with a failure rate of about 15% — per search summary (source: [Copper Alliance](https://copperindia.org/casestudies/distribution-transformer-repair-report/))
- AT&C losses fell from 21.91% (FY2021) to 16.16% (FY2025) — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2200450&reg=3&lang=2); [Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/india-reduces-atc-losses-to-1616-in-fy25-power-ministry-10894965). **Conflict:** the Ministry of Power on X and T&D India report 15.04% for FY2025 — [MoP on X](https://x.com/MinOfPower/status/2020753164992131580); [T&D India](https://www.tndindia.com/overall-atc-losses-of-distribution-utilities-improve-to-15-04-per-cent-in-fy25/). The difference is probably methodology (PFC report vs RDSS computation). Cite "about 15–16%" or name the source.
- The RDSS target is 12–15% pan-India AT&C losses. 5.28 crore smart meters have been deployed — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2200450&reg=3&lang=2)

### Inferences
- Evening overloading of DTs from cooling loads, combined with unmanaged rooftop backfeed, raises thermal stress. A battery at the DT that shaves the local peak can extend asset life.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or CEA data was found linking DT failures to peak overloading specifically.

## 7. Battery costs, BESS tenders, VGF and installed storage

### Takeaway
Storage has become cheap fast. Stationary packs average about $70/kWh globally (2025), India's BESS tariffs fell to Rs 1.48–1.78 lakh/MW/month, and VGF tranche 2 is live. Installed BESS jumped from almost nothing to about 9–12 GWh in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- BNEF 2025 survey: average Li-ion pack $108/kWh, LFP packs $81/kWh, stationary storage about $70/kWh (−45% YoY) — [ESS News, Dec 2025](https://www.ess-news.com/2025/12/09/bnef-lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-kwh-stationary-storage-becomes-lowest-price-segment/)
- India BESS capex benchmark used by IEEFA tariff analysis: about USD 115/kWh all-inclusive — [IEEFA, May 2026](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/Viability%20of%20standalone%20battery%20energy%20storage%20tariffs%20discovered%20in%202025.pdf)
- Vendor estimates for 2026 (lower credibility): LFP cells USD 65–80/kWh CIF India (about Rs 5,500–6,900/kWh). Utility BESS installed capex Rs 1.3–1.8 crore/MWh (Rs 13,000–18,000/kWh) — [Silicindia Energies](https://www.silicindiaenergies.com/bess-cost-india/); [Alpha Devraj](https://alphadevrajess.com/resources/bess-price-india)
- Rajasthan RVUNL 1 GW/2 GWh standalone BESS (Oct 2025, VGF tranche 2) cleared at Rs 1.775–1.785 lakh/MW/month, 15% below the previous L1, with VGF of Rs 18 lakh/MWh — [Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/rajasthans-first-1gw2gwh-bess-tender-cleared-at-record-rs-1775-lakhmw-10540181); [Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/rajasthan-awards-1gw2gwh-standalone-bess-tender-at-record-low-tariffs); [pv magazine India](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2025/10/09/rajasthans-1-gw-2-gwh-battery-storage-tender-sets-new-tariff-benchmark-at-1999-45-mw-month/)
- Reported national low: Rs 1.48 lakh/MW/month (SVR Electro Projects, APTRANSCO 1,000 MW, Dec 2025) — [MVApulse tracker](https://www.mvapulse.com/bess-tender-tracker/) (aggregator; verify against Mercom/JMK). Haryana: NLC India Renewables 265 MW at Rs 2.35 lakh/MW/month — [Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/nlc-india-renewables-win-265-mw-bess-tender-in-haryana-at-rs-235-lacsmwmonth-12573280)
- Installed BESS was 5.9 GWh as of Mar 2026, after a record 4.6 GWh added in Q1 2026 ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/india-s-installed-battery-energy-storage-capacity-touches-5-9-gwh-adds-record-4-6-gwh-in-1st-quarter-of-2026)). India added 8.2 GWh in H1 2026, about 84x the 98.4 MWh added in H1 2025 ([pv magazine, Sep 2026](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/14/india-adds-8-2-gwh-of-new-bess-capacity-in-h1/); [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/storage/india-h1-2026-battery-storage-installations)). Sources disagree on the cumulative total for mid-2026, reporting 8.5 or 9.3 GWh.
- Need: over 411.4 GWh of BESS by 2031-32 (CEA estimate, as quoted in press) — [Down To Earth / Business Standard coverage](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/decoding-indias-record-256-gw-power-peak-demand-dispatch-and-dilemmas)

### Inferences
- At about Rs 1.5–1.8 lakh/MW/month, a 1 MW/2 MWh battery costs a DISCOM about Rs 18–21 lakh a year. That is a useful anchor for pricing a neighbourhood-scale (100–500 kWh) community battery.

### Gaps
- No credible India-specific data was found on second-life EV battery deployment for stationary storage. Avoid cost claims about it.

## 8. Community / distribution-level storage pilots in India

### Takeaway
Delhi DISCOMs have already shown distribution-level storage, from a 0.52 MWh community battery to a 20 MW/40 MWh substation BESS serving about 100,000 residents. Neighbourhood-scale storage is technically proven in India and has regulatory precedent.

### Cited Findings
- **TPDDL + Nexcharge, Rani Bagh CESS (Mar 2021) [OLDER]:** India's first grid-connected Li-ion Community Energy Storage System, 0.52 MWh. It provides peak shaving, VAR compensation and DSM-frequency response, plus backup to "preferential consumers" during grid outages — [Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/nexcharge-tata-power-ddl-sets-up-indias-1st-grid-connected-li-ion-battery-based-cess-in-delhi); [TPDDL DER page](https://www.tatapower-ddl.com/corporate/smart-grid-index/DER)
- **TPDDL 10 MW BESS, Rohini (with AES and Mitsubishi) [OLDER, 2019]:** South Asia's first 10 MW grid-scale BESS, used for demand-side management, frequency regulation and supply reliability to key consumers — [TPDDL DER](https://www.tatapower-ddl.com/corporate/smart-grid-index/DER); [TPDDL press release](https://tatapower-ddl.com/pr-details/199/1825286/delhi-power-minister-visits-south-asia%E2%80%99s-largest-battery-energy-storage-system;-appreciates-tata-power-ddl-for-innovative-project-in-delhi). An ADB grant was also pledged for a TPDDL BESS pilot — [Energy-Storage.News](https://www.energy-storage.news/asian-development-bank-pledges-grant-funding-for-tata-power-delhi-distribution-battery-storage-pilot/)
- **BRPL Kilokari 20 MW/40 MWh (commissioned 31 Mar 2025):** India's first commercial standalone BESS at distribution level, and the first approved under Section 63. Built by IndiGrid and AmpereHour in 11 months, with GEAPP providing a concessional loan covering 70% of cost. It gives 4 hours a day of peak support (2 h afternoon, 2 h night) and benefits about 100,000 South Delhi residents — [pv magazine India](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2025/05/06/amperehour-commissions-20-mw-40-mwh-bess-in-delhi/); [GEAPP](https://energyalliance.org/india-first-utility-scale-standalone-battery-energy-storage-system/); [IndiGrid](https://www.indigrid.co.in/portfolio-assets/kilokari-bess-private-limited-kbpl/). BRPL plans further BESS expansion — [Daily Pioneer, 2025](https://www.dailypioneer.com/2025/state-editions/brpl-gears-up-to-expand-battery-storage-system-across-key-areas.html)
- Tata Power BESS supporting critical infrastructure in Mumbai — [Energy-Storage.News](https://www.energy-storage.news/tata-power-deploys-battery-storage-to-support-critical-infrastructure-in-mumbai-india/)

### Inferences
- The Rani Bagh CESS's "backup to preferential consumers" is a direct precedent for GridKavach's critical-load prioritisation. Kilokari shows the DISCOM-procured, tariff-based business model works.

### Gaps
- No published quantitative outcomes (reduction in outage minutes, peak shaved, savings) were found for Rani Bagh or Kilokari.
- Smart Power India / Prayas mini-grid outcomes were not researched (time budget).
