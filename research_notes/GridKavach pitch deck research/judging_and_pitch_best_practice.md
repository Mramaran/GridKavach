# Yuva Yodha Energy Tech Hackathon 2026: Judging Rules, Screening and Pitch-Deck Best Practice (Challenge 03, Grid Reliability)

Research date: 2026-10-01 (idea-submission deadline is 2026-10-04, 11:59 PM IST).
Labels: **[OFFICIAL]** = stated on yuvayodhatech.com or in Schneider press coverage. **[THIRD-PARTY]** = aggregator or participant page, not verified against an official source. **[BEST PRACTICE]** = general guidance from other hackathons.

## What are the official evaluation/judging criteria and their weights?

### Takeaway
The official FAQ publishes weighted criteria: Problem Understanding & Idea Quality 20%, Architecture & Design 20%, Impact & Measurability 25%, Feasibility & Affordability 20%, Sustainability 15%. Judges apply them with "Indian realities" and real-world deployment constraints in mind. The Wooble/YouNoodle listing gives a different six-criterion list with no weights. The deck should satisfy both, with the FAQ weights treated as the primary rubric.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] FAQ criteria and weights: Problem Understanding & Idea Quality (20%), Architecture & Design (20%), Impact & Measurability (25%), Feasibility & Affordability (20%), Sustainability (15%). Assessments consider "Indian realities" and real-world deployment constraints. — [yuvayodhatech.com/faq](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/faq)
- [THIRD-PARTY, listing page] The Wooble/YouNoodle page lists six unweighted criteria: problem understanding; innovation and creativity; technical feasibility and prototype; energy and sustainability impact; scalability and practical implementation; presentation and communication. — [Wooble listing](https://wooble.org/companies/wooble-programs/challenges/yuva-yodha-energy-tech-hackathon-2026). This conflicts with the official FAQ, which has five weighted criteria and no separate "innovation" or "presentation" line.
- [OFFICIAL] The Terms & Conditions mention merit-based selection but do not set out criteria or a method. Sponsor and administrator decisions are "final and binding in all respects." — [yuvayodhatech.com/terms](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/terms)
- [OFFICIAL] The Challenge 03 page names these expected deliverables:
  1. A detailed write-up covering the mechanism, assumptions and fit with Indian distribution conditions.
  2. A system architecture diagram showing components and data, energy and money flows.
  3. Design artifacts such as UX wireframes, process designs or data models.
  4. A software prototype or simulation. This is encouraged but not mandatory, and no physical hardware is expected.
  5. Measurable reliability metrics, such as outage reduction or improvement in supply availability against a baseline.
  6. An operations model covering local ownership and maintenance, with unit economics that show affordability.

  Source: [yuvayodhatech.com/challenges](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/challenges)
- [OFFICIAL] Challenge 03 focus areas:
  - Bridge intermittency gaps when renewable supply falls below demand.
  - Deploy at neighbourhood, district or feeder level with minimal grid-level intervention ("local, low-cost flexibility tools" with no major grid reinforcement).
  - Stay affordable for low-income urban and peri-urban communities.
  - Give DISCOMs data and demand-response capability.

  Keywords: community or shared storage (second-life batteries), microgrid or mini-grid control software, peer-to-peer and cooperative energy sharing, forecasting and load shifting, smart demand response, feeder-level flexibility. Source: [yuvayodhatech.com/challenges](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/challenges)

### Inferences
- How the weights map to the 8-12 slides:
  - Impact & Measurability (25%) and the Challenge 03 "measurable reliability metrics against a baseline" requirement point to a dedicated quantified-impact slide. Useful metrics: SAIDI/SAIFI-style outage hours avoided, % supply availability, kWh shifted, ₹ saved, CO2 avoided.
  - Feasibility & Affordability (20%) maps to a unit-economics and operations-model slide (₹ per household per month, payback period, who owns and maintains the system).
  - Architecture & Design (20%) maps to one clean architecture slide showing data, energy and money flows, plus a wireframe or user-journey slide.
  - Sustainability (15%) should cover both environmental benefit (more renewable absorbed, less diesel-genset use) and long-term operation (local ownership, maintenance).
- Feasibility & Affordability (20%) and Sustainability (15%) together make up 35% of the score. Business and operations realism therefore counts as much as technical novelty.
- The emphasis on "Indian realities" means the deck should name Indian specifics, for example:
  - DISCOM structures and finances
  - Feeder-level outages
  - Time-of-day tariffs
  - Rooftop-solar programmes such as PM Surya Ghar (this example was not taken from an official page)
  - Low-income affordability

### Gaps
- No official per-criterion scoring scale (for example 1-10) or official rubric descriptors were found.
- The FAQ weights may apply to the whole competition or only to some stages. The site does not say whether idea screening uses the same weights as the prototype and finale stages.
- The HackerEarth registration page was not reviewed. The official FAQ says registration runs through YouNoodle, so HackerEarth may not be an official channel.

## What are the exact presentation requirements and the format, IP, AI-use and eligibility rules?

### Takeaway
I could not find the 8-12 slide / 300-500 word requirement on any public page. It is most likely in the YouNoodle submission form, which requires a login. No official slide template, file-size limit or font rule was found. Official rules that do affect the deck:
- Participants keep their IP but grant Schneider a perpetual, non-exclusive, royalty-free licence.
- "Unauthorized AI-generated content" and plagiarism are prohibited.
- Each person may be on one team and one submission only.
- Submissions can be edited and resubmitted until the deadline.

### Inferences
- The slide list in the brief maps onto the official criteria as follows:
  - Problem statement and alignment to the challenge: Problem Understanding (20%)
  - Solution and innovation: Idea Quality (20%)
  - Technical approach and features / user journey: Architecture & Design (20%)
  - Expected impact: Impact & Measurability (25%)
  - Roadmap, operations and costs: Feasibility & Affordability (20%)
  - Sustainability: 15%

  Sustainability has no dedicated slide in the required list, so it should be added as an explicit block, either on the impact slide or as its own slide.
- Because of the perpetual licence, do not put trade secrets in the deck that you would not want Schneider to reuse.
- Because of the AI clause, avoid AI-generated imagery or text that could look unoriginal. Cite data sources, and consider adding a short statement about tools used.
- Export to PDF to keep the layout intact, and check the YouNoodle form for limits before Oct 4. Treat this as best practice; no official size rule was found.

## What do Schneider Electric judges value, based on Go Green, SE Ventures and executive statements?

### Takeaway
Schneider's long-running student competition, Go Green, consistently judges on innovation, feasibility, sustainable impact and the financial or business case, with "Affordable, Sustainable, Innovative, Local" as its design tests. SE Ventures invests along three themes: electrification, digitization and decarbonization, including grid modernization and resilience. A deck that pairs grid impact with a credible business model and fits the digital, EcoStruxure-style approach matches these patterns.

### Cited Findings
- [SCHNEIDER, other competition] Go Green 2026 asks for:
  - A clearly defined energy gap
  - A viable business model with a financial model
  - Efficiency gains in energy cost or productivity
  - Local impact on jobs, education and community
  - Digital enablement
  - Community co-design

  Projects must be "Affordable," "Sustainable," "Innovative," and "Local." Its stages are: submission, top 10, a mentored development phase, top 3, then virtual finals. — [gogreencompetition.com](https://www.gogreencompetition.com/)
- [SCHNEIDER, earlier Go Green editions, via aggregator] Judging by stage:
  - First stage: innovativeness, feasibility, presentation quality and sustainable impact.
  - Semi-final: understanding of user needs, innovation, feasibility and financial business case, and sustainable impact.
  - Finals: feasibility, profitability, innovation and sustainable impact.

  Source: summary of [Go Green 2021 FAQ](https://gogreen.se.com/en/challenges/2021-europe/pages/faqs?lang=en) and [drivesandcontrols.ca](https://www.drivesandcontrols.ca/latest-articles/schneider-electrics-go-green-competition-a-world-wide-opportunity-to-ignite-change/). These details came from search-result summaries and were not read in full.
- [SE Ventures] SE Ventures invests along three pillars: Electrification, Digitization and Decarbonization. Its themes include grid modernization and resilience, smart buildings and industrial automation, and it places Schneider Operating Partners in each portfolio company. — [crunchbase news](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/schneider-ai-robotics-energy-qa-chaturvedy-se-ventures/), [vcsheet](https://www.vcsheet.com/fund/se-ventures), [seventures.com/team](https://www.seventures.com/team/)
- [OFFICIAL press] Deepak Sharma speaks of turning bold ideas into "tangible impact" for India's energy transition. Niharika Mohan describes the programme as central to Schneider's "business and people strategy," and it doubles as a recruitment pipeline through PPIs. — [itvoice.in](https://www.itvoice.in/schneider-electric-launches-yuva-yodha-aims-to-accelerate-youth-led-innovation-to-power-indias-energy-transition)

### Inferences
- Across Schneider competitions, judges reward the same few things: a quantified local problem, a digital solution, affordability and unit economics, measurable sustainability impact, and a path to scale. A purely technical deck with no money flows or ownership model is likely to underperform.
- Mentioning integration points with grid and DER platforms (for example DERMS/ADMS, microgrid controllers or Schneider's EcoStruxure Grid and Microgrid offerings) can show alignment. The deck must stay vendor-neutral and original, and should not imply any Schneider endorsement.
- Since PPIs are part of the prize, a team slide that shows relevant skills (power systems, ML/forecasting, software, economics) supports both feasibility and Schneider's recruitment goal.

## What makes idea-stage hackathon pitch decks pass screening and win? (General best practice)

### Takeaway
Screening decks most often fail for four reasons: they miss stated requirements, they misread the problem, they make vague or unquantified claims, or they are cluttered. Decks that pass map their content directly to the published criteria, quantify impact, show a clear architecture and demo or wireframe, and keep slides scannable.

### Cited Findings
- [BEST PRACTICE] Devpost judges:
  - Projects that stand out "clearly considered the judging criteria."
  - Common mistakes are ignoring requirements, uneven effort (polished UI on a weak core), vague descriptions, recycled ideas and unclear purpose.
  - Storytelling and detailed write-ups help.

  Source: [Devpost: advice from 5 judges](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- [BEST PRACTICE] Typical criteria are technical implementation, ease of use, demo, potential impact, idea quality and design. Common disqualifications are failing eligibility, missing technical requirements, a poor demo and ignoring the theme. "If you can't explain how to use your project in a sentence or two, it might be too complicated." — [Devpost: submission and judging criteria](https://info.devpost.com/blog/understanding-hackathon-submission-and-judging-criteria)
- [BEST PRACTICE] Focus on the idea and its impact. Solve one main problem with a few supporting features, and submit early. — [Devpost tips via Medium](https://medium.com/developer-circles-lusaka/tips-on-how-to-win-an-online-hackathon-on-devpost-c548027e0eae)
- [BEST PRACTICE, India] Indian idea-PPT rounds (Unstop, SIH) usually require sections for problem, proposed solution, innovation, technical approach or architecture, feasibility, implementation and impact. Slide caps are often enforced, and over-limit or template-breaking decks risk disqualification. Reviewers want short bullets, one diagram per idea, and a clear title on every slide. — [Unstop hackathon listings via search](https://unstop.com/hackathons/hack-in-hills-26-galgotias-university-gu-greater-noida-1752595), [reskilll SIH guide](https://reskilll.com/blogs/smart-india-hackathon-2026-complete-guide-registration-themes-winning/)
- [BEST PRACTICE, India, unofficial blog] A reskilll blog lists SIH judging weights as problem understanding 30%, technical 25%, innovation 20%, feasibility/scalability 15% and presentation 10%. Common rejection reasons it gives are solving the wrong problem, ignoring stated constraints and incomplete documentation. These weights are not from an official SIH source. — [reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/smart-india-hackathon-2026-complete-guide-registration-themes-winning/)

### Inferences
Suggested 12-slide structure that maps to the official Yuva Yodha weights:
1. **Title and one-line value proposition.** Name Challenge 03, Grid Reliability: Renewable Intermittency.
2. **Problem, quantified for India.** Cover feeder outages, evening solar drop-off and DISCOM stress, using cited data. (Problem Understanding, 20%)
3. **Who suffers and the current gaps.** Cover low-income urban and peri-urban users, DISCOMs, and why diesel gensets and grid reinforcement fail. (Problem)
4. **Proposed solution and alignment.** Map explicitly to each Challenge 03 focus area: intermittency, local deployment, affordability, DISCOM data and demand response. (Idea Quality)
5. **Key features and user journey.** Show wireframes for household, community operator and DISCOM views. (Architecture & Design)
6. **Technical architecture.** Use one diagram with data, energy and money flows, which the challenge requires. (Architecture & Design, 20%)
7. **Innovation and differentiation.** Compare against existing approaches. (Idea Quality)
8. **Impact and measurability.** Show baseline vs target for outage hours, availability %, kWh shifted, renewable curtailment avoided and CO2. State the method and assumptions. (Impact, 25%)
9. **Affordability and operations model.** Cover unit economics in ₹, local ownership and maintenance, and revenue or tariff model. (Feasibility & Affordability, 20%)
10. **Sustainability and scale.** Cover second-life batteries and circularity, a replication path across feeders and states, and the SE Ventures angle. (Sustainability, 15%)
11. **Implementation roadmap.** Show prototype or simulation milestones for Oct 11 to Nov 22, then pilot and scale-up. (Feasibility)
12. **Team introduction.** Show roles and relevant skills.

Other recommendations:
- The 300-500 word description should repeat the same skeleton: problem, solution, how it works, measurable impact, affordability, and why it fits India.
- Use the official keywords (feeder-level flexibility, demand response, forecasting, community storage) so screeners can match the submission to the challenge quickly.
