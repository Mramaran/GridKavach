# Build GridKavach's deck around the 25% impact weight

GridKavach should go in as a 12-slide deck that follows the official Yuva Yodha rubric closely: Problem Understanding & Idea Quality 20%, Architecture & Design 20%, Impact & Measurability 25%, Feasibility & Affordability 20%, Sustainability 15%, judged against "Indian realities" ([Yuva Yodha FAQ](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/faq)). The current concept spec already covers most of the six Challenge 03 deliverables. It has three gaps. Sustainability, worth 15%, has no slide. The roadmap is not tied to the Oct 11 to Nov 22 prototype window that screening admits teams into. Several headline claims need correcting before they can face a technical judge. The research supports a sharp problem line ("India no longer runs short at 3 pm; it runs short at 10:45 pm") and a corrected smart-meter base (20.33 crore sanctioned under RDSS, 7.24 crore installed nationally, not "22+ crore"). It also supports an honest novelty position. Every individual GridKavach module has prior art: Eskom load limiting, TPDDL's community battery, AMI last-gasp outage management, UK Smart ReStart, and Siemens and Schneider ADMS/DERMS. What appears new is the combination. That combination is a DER-interlocked permit-to-work, community-governed fair-access rules on a shared battery, and tiered essential power, all aimed at grid-connected low-income Indian feeders. The deck should lead with that combination and present the individual modules as proven building blocks. The team should not build slides until about a dozen open items are checked, above all the live YouNoodle form fields, because the 8-12 slide / 300-500 word format could not be confirmed on any public page.

## Five weighted criteria decide screening, and 35% of the score is business realism

The official FAQ is the primary rubric: **Problem Understanding & Idea Quality 20%, Architecture & Design 20%, Impact & Measurability 25%, Feasibility & Affordability 20%, Sustainability 15%**, with assessment against "Indian realities" and real-world deployment constraints ([FAQ](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/faq)). A third-party listing gives six different, unweighted criteria: problem understanding, innovation, technical feasibility and prototype, energy and sustainability impact, scalability, and presentation ([Wooble](https://wooble.org/companies/wooble-programs/challenges/yuva-yodha-energy-tech-hackathon-2026)). The safe approach is to score on the FAQ weights and make innovation and presentation obvious along the way. No official scoring scale or rubric descriptors were found. The site also does not say whether idea screening uses the same weights as later stages.

The Challenge 03 page lists six expected deliverables ([Challenges](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/challenges)):

1. A write-up covering mechanism, assumptions and fit with Indian distribution conditions.
2. An architecture diagram showing **data, energy and money flows**.
3. Design artifacts: wireframes, process designs or data models.
4. An optional software prototype or simulation.
5. **Measurable reliability metrics against a baseline**.
6. An operations model with local ownership, maintenance and unit economics.

The focus areas are bridging intermittency gaps, deploying at neighbourhood or feeder level without major grid reinforcement, staying affordable for low-income urban and peri-urban users, and giving DISCOMs data and demand-response capability. The listed keywords are worth using verbatim so screeners can match the submission quickly: community or shared storage (second-life), microgrid control, cooperative energy sharing, forecasting and load shifting, smart demand response, feeder-level flexibility.

The process runs as follows ([yuvayodhatech.com](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/)):

| Prototype and judging (shortlisted teams) | **Oct 11 to Nov 22** |

The top 10 present in person at Schneider's Bengaluru office and get pre-placement interviews and exposure to SE Ventures ([Terms](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/terms)). Prizes are ₹20/15/10 lakh. The shortlist size after the idea stage is not published.

Three rules affect the deck:

- Participants keep their IP but grant Schneider a **perpetual, royalty-free licence** to use the submission, so leave out anything you would not want reused.
- The Terms prohibit "unauthorized AI-generated content" without defining it ([Terms](https://www.yuvayodhatech.com/terms)). Keep sourced figures, original diagrams and a short tools statement.
- Teams can un-submit and edit until the deadline but not after.

Schneider's other student competition, Go Green, consistently rewards a defined energy gap, a financial model, local community impact and digital enablement, under the tests "Affordable, Sustainable, Innovative, Local" ([Go Green](https://www.gogreencompetition.com/)). SE Ventures invests in grid modernisation and resilience under electrification, digitization and decarbonization ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/schneider-ai-robotics-energy-qa-chaturvedy-se-ventures/)). A deck with no money flows or ownership model will underperform, whatever its technical quality.

Slide 8 carries the largest weight and the most risk. The deck must not invent outcome numbers. The spec itself says "report only numbers the simulator produces." If the simulator cannot produce results before Oct 4, the slide should give the metric definitions, the baseline, the method and **target hypotheses** labelled as targets. External benchmarks (Eskom about 1 kW per meter, IEEE 210% cold-load factor) show the targets are grounded. A target-labelled slide passes screening. A fabricated "47% fewer outage hours" invites disqualification under the plagiarism and AI-content clause and destroys credibility with a technical panel.

Slide 9 needs one illustrative calculation, not a full model. Using the spec formula with stated assumptions (400 households × 150 W essential tier × 3 gap hours ÷ 0.8 depth of discharge) gives a battery of about **225 kWh**. A utility-scale tariff floor can then be derived from the Rajasthan tender: Rs 1.775 lakh/MW/month for 2 MWh works out to about Rs 10.7 lakh per MWh per year. That puts a 225 kWh battery at roughly Rs 2.4 lakh a year, about **Rs 50 per household per month** before operator wage, licence and small-scale cost premiums. This is a team calculation from a sourced tariff, not a sourced figure. The tariff includes viability-gap funding and gigawatt-scale economics, so the slide must present it as a floor. A neighbourhood battery will cost several times more per kWh. The headline comparison ("less than a private inverter costs one home") still has no sourced inverter price, and the research explicitly warns against citing the commonly quoted Rs 15,000–30,000 without one.

The 300–500 word description should follow the deck's sequence so the two read as one submission. A workable budget is about 60 words of problem (night deficit, low-income feeder, crew safety), 100 on the three layers, 60 on architecture and the three flows, 80 on measurable impact against baseline, 80 on ownership and unit economics, 50 on sustainability, and 40 on prior-art positioning and the prototype plan. It should include the official keywords (feeder-level flexibility, community storage, demand response, forecasting and load shifting). It should use the same corrected figures as the slides, because a mismatch between deck and text is an easy screening flag.

## The novelty is the governed combination, not any single module

Every GridKavach module has close prior art. A Schneider judge will know this, partly because some of it is Schneider's own product line.

- **Siemens Gridscale X Flexibility Manager** already forecasts overloads and identifies flexibility from batteries and distributed generation ([Siemens](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-unveils-flexibility-software-increase-electricity-grid-capacity-moving-towards)). Schneider's EcoStruxure ADMS ships with embedded DERMS ([Schneider blog](https://blog.se.com/energy-management-energy-efficiency/2026/07/03/unlocking-grid-efficiency-and-roi-findings-from-a-forrester-total-economic-impact-study-of-schneider-electrics-ecostruxure-adms-with-embedded-derms/)). Its GridOps suite includes Switching Management and Switching Validations ([Schneider ADMS 3.10](https://smartgrid.schneider-electric.com/s/blog-article/a05QO00000EiCVFYA3/adms-310-hf9-product-documentation-update)).
- **AMI last-gasp outage localisation** to the service transformer is mature OMS practice ([Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/power-grid/outage-management/enhancing-outage-management-with-ami/)). Tata Power-DDL runs ADMS with automated FLISR in Delhi ([TPDDL](https://www.tatapower-ddl.com/corporate/smart-grid-index/monitoring-control)).
- **UK Project Smart ReStart** staggers smart-meter reconnection to cut the restoration spike ([LCP](https://www.lcp.com/en/insights/blogs/project-smart-restart-how-smart-meters-can-help-to-strengthen-local-electricity-networks)). A US patent covers cold-load management via demand response ([US 8600573](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8600573)).
- **Eskom** rolled out 10 A smart-meter load limiting nationally in January 2024 after a pilot of 12,506 meters ([SAGEN](https://www.cityenergy.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Eskom-Load-limiting-pilot.pdf)). **TPDDL's Rani Bagh CESS** already backs up "preferential consumers" from a community battery ([Mercom India](https://www.mercomindia.com/tata-power-nexcharge-battery-community-energy-storage)).
- **SparkMeter** offers group-wise load limits in mini-grids ([Engineering for Change](https://www.engineeringforchange.org/solutions/product/sparkmeter/)). Academic work on fair and "soft" load shedding through AMI quotas is extensive ([arXiv 2002.00451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00451); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-019-09428-8)).

The novelty claims that survive, from strongest to weakest:

1. **SafeLine.** A permit is released only after automated discovery of back-feed sources and telemetry confirmation that their export is blocked. No surveyed product does this, and it fits the documented Indian failure mode of wrongful or multi-source re-energisation.
2. **Community-governed fair-access dispatch of a shared battery during shortages.** Each home gets an essential floor and institutions get priority, under an RWA/SHG owner with a DISCOM licence. Australian community batteries share through tariffs, not shortage rules ([Ausgrid](https://www.ausgrid.com.au/About-Us/News/Ausgrid-community-batteries-unlock-significant-savings-for-customers)). Indian ones are DISCOM-owned.
3. **Tiered essential-load rationing aimed at low-income neighbourhoods.** This addresses Eskom's documented equity failure: affluent Fourways got load limiting, while dense, poorer Soweto got harsher "load reduction" ([Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-11-10-soweto-residents-decry-ongoing-power-cuts-under-eskoms-load-reduction-programme/)).
4. **The integrated feeder-scale bundle,** priced for community operators rather than enterprise control rooms.

Even the strongest claim should be worded as "not found in surveyed products." The researchers could not inspect the detailed permit features of Schneider, Siemens, GE Vernova or Hitachi ADMS, and no Indian patent search was done.

This framing helps the score. The fairness literature becomes theory supporting Flex rather than a threat to it. The Eskom data gives slide 8 a real external benchmark. Showing TPDDL and Kilokari as precedents tells judges that the technology is proven in India, which strengthens the Feasibility score instead of weakening the Innovation score. Slide 7 should therefore use a feature matrix that marks prior art honestly and highlights only the columns where GridKavach is alone.

## Conclusion

The research changes the pitch's centre of gravity. The concept spec treats the three Kavach modules as roughly equal inventions. The evidence says two of them (LastGasp and ColdStart) are established utility practice that GridKavach adapts. The third (SafeLine) is the one component without a surveyed equivalent, but only if it is framed around multi-source back-feed and wrongful re-energisation rather than rooftop solar. The deck should therefore stop saying "we invented smarter grid protection." It should say "we package proven tools for a segment nobody serves, and add the governance and safety interlock that make them safe to hand to a community." That message also fits a Schneider audience that already sells ADMS, because it presents GridKavach as a low-voltage community extension rather than a competitor.

The biggest scoring risk is not novelty but evidence. Impact & Measurability is the heaviest criterion, and GridKavach currently has a well-defined method but no results. Honest, target-labelled metrics with external anchors (Eskom's about 1 kW per meter, IEEE's 210% cold-load factor) and a credible six-week plan to produce real numbers are the best available position for screening. Producing those numbers is the main reason to win a place in the Oct 11 to Nov 22 phase.
